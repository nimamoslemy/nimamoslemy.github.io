Build a lattice Read the publications

Everything starts with one cell.

This is the body-centred cubic (BCC) unit cell: eight struts meeting at a single node.

Repeat it in three directions and you get a metal part that is mostly empty space but still carries load. My PhD asks how far the printed lattice drifts from the designed one, and what that costs in stiffness and strength.

Process
Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF)
Structure
BCC strut lattices
Methods
Analytical models, FEA, XCT, mechanical testing
Funding
EPSRC doctoral programme

Printed from powder, 30 microns at a time.

In LPBF, a laser melts a thin layer of metal powder, the bed drops, and the next layer goes on top. Keep scrolling to run the build.

Laser power, scan speed and hatch spacing decide what each strut really looks like. Small changes in the process show up later as big changes in the part.

What comes out is never the CAD.

X-ray computed tomography (XCT) shows the real struts: wavy, thicker or thinner than designed, rough on the surface and porous inside.

I measure these deviations strut by strut and put them back into the simulation, so the model describes the part that was printed and not the one that was drawn.

Then it gets crushed.

Compression tests and finite element analysis show where a BCC lattice gives way first: in bending, right next to the nodes.

Analytical models turn that into a prediction of stiffness. The long-term goal is a dataset that links manufacturing parameters to mechanical performance, ready for machine learning.

About me.

I am Nima Moslemy, a PhD researcher in mechanical engineering at Heriot-Watt University in Edinburgh, on an EPSRC-funded programme.

My work joins metal 3D printing with simulation and software: laser powder bed fusion, architected lattice structures, finite element analysis, and machine learning for defect detection.

Before the PhD I studied biomechanical engineering at Amirkabir University of Technology and led a hospital manufacturing team that made more than 600 patient-specific prostheses.
